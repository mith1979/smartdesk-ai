# NovaTech Solutions — Reporting Suspicious Email

This is a fictional guide generated for the SmartDesk AI capstone.
All company rules, contacts, and processes are demonstration values.

## Recognizing a Concern
Unexpected requests for passwords, MFA codes, urgent payments, or unfamiliar sign-in links should be treated as suspicious. A familiar sender name alone does not establish that a message is safe. Employees should report concerns even when they are unsure whether the email is malicious.

## Reporting Without Interacting
Do not open unexpected attachments, follow suspicious links, reply, or approve an unexpected MFA request. Use the Report Phishing action in the company email application when available. If it is unavailable, contact helpdesk@novatech.example with the sender address, subject, received time, and a short description. Do not paste credentials, attachment contents, or sensitive message bodies into SmartDesk.

## If You Already Interacted
Contact IT immediately if you clicked a suspicious link, opened an attachment, entered credentials, or approved an unexpected verification request. Explain which action occurred and when. Use the existing Password Reset Guide if IT directs you to reset your employee password. Do not assume that resetting a password alone resolves a possible compromise.

## Handling the Message
Do not forward the suspicious email to colleagues or continue investigating it yourself. Follow IT instructions for providing the original message through an approved channel. Preserve the received time and subject so IT can identify the report.

## Support and Limits
This is an employee reporting guide, not an incident-response or forensic procedure. IT determines subsequent actions. SmartDesk cannot verify that a message is safe, inspect attachments, guarantee containment, or promise a resolution deadline.
